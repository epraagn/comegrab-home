AI Application: The Price of a Question

We built a Q&A bot over a set of PDFs, then asked the questions that matter for any AI product: what does one answer cost, who caused it, and how do we stop it running away?

A hands-on lab with a document Q&A bot · 8 items

Output was 29% of the tokens and 68% of the bill. The answer was also cut off at 500 tokens.

Every AI feature has a meter running behind it. A person types a question, a few hundred words travel to a model, a few hundred come back, and somebody gets billed for both directions.

This part covers the working foundation: how the bot finds an answer, what that answer costs, how we record it, and the first limits that keep a bug or a busy user from draining the account. Each item has a revision table, one rule to remember, and where it helps, a calculator you can play with. Throughout, we use one running example: a single call with 1,196 input tokens and 500 output tokens.

Item 1 · RAG pipeline

Fetch first, then ask

Claude has never read your documents. Sending whole documents with every question can cost tens of thousands of tokens a time. Sending only the four most relevant chunks costs about 1,200.

Phase A · indexing, once, free

  1. Extract text
  2. Chunk
  3. Embed
  4. Store in ChromaDB

Phase B · every question, costs money

  1. Embed question
  2. Find top 4 chunks
  3. Build prompt
  4. Claude answers
  5. Log cost
What it isRetrieval-Augmented Generation. Find the relevant text first, then let Claude answer from it.
Indexingpypdf extracts text, which is cut into 800-character chunks with 100 characters of overlap. Each chunk is embedded and stored. It runs on your PC and costs nothing.
QueryingThe question is embedded the same way, the 4 closest chunks are found, a prompt is built ("answer using only this context"), Claude answers, and the call is logged.
Key termsChunk: a small piece of text. Embedding: numbers that capture meaning. Vector DB: searches by meaning, not keywords. Top-k: how many chunks are sent.
Why overlapA sentence sitting on a chunk boundary is not cut in half.
Tuning knobsChunk size, overlap, TOP_K, MAX_TOKENS. Raising k gives better recall at higher cost.
Example numbersIn our example, 29 pages became 125 chunks. About 1,200 tokens are sent per question.
RememberIf retrieval picks the wrong chunks, Claude answers wrong even though Claude itself is fine.

Item 2 · Tokens and cost per call

Tokens: the meter that is always running

A token is a piece of text about three or four characters long. Billing counts tokens in both directions, and the two directions are priced differently.

Two metersInput: everything you send (instruction, chunks, question), $1 per million. Output: everything Claude writes, $5 per million. These are placeholder prices; check the pricing page.
Formulain / 1M × in_price + out / 1M × out_price
Example call1,196 in = $0.0012. 500 out = $0.0025. Total $0.0037.
Key insightOutput was 29% of the tokens but 68% of the cost.
Hidden costsThinking tokens count as output. Retrieved chunks repeat on every call. Retries are billed. Cut-off answers are billed too.
Scale effectAt $0.0032 a question: 100 a day is about $10 a month, 10,000 a day is about $960 a month, 100,000 a day is about $9,600 a month.
Cost leversFewer or shorter chunks, shorter answers, a cheaper model for easy questions, caching, prompt caching.

Item 3 · Logging

Keep the receipts

Every question becomes one row in usage.db. Reports, budgets, quotas and charts are all different ways of reading that one table.

What we storeTime, user, question, model, input and output tokens, input and output cost, total cost, latency, stop reason, and an is_demo flag.
PrincipleLog raw facts once, then derive many views from them.
What it powersThe report command, budget left, daily cap and user quota, top-5 costly questions, cost per user, cut-off rate, the audit tab.
Design choicesCost is stored per row because prices change. Demo rows are flagged so fake data never touches a real budget. Old tables upgrade themselves. The row is written after the call because tokens are only known then.
Not logged yetWhich chunks were retrieved, the answer text, time per step, prompt version, trace ID, errors and retries.
Privacy riskQuestions can contain names and other personal details, especially in sensitive areas such as legal or health topics. Mask personal data before saving and set a retention period.
RememberYou cannot log the past. Decide now what you will want to slice by later.

Item 4 · Budgets and projections

Budgets, and the forecast that lies politely

A budget asks how much you have spent. A projection asks how much you will spend. The second one is only as good as the average underneath it.

FormulasAverage = total / calls. Projection = average × volume. Runway = budget left / average.
Core formulamonthly cost = users × questions per user × cost per question
Example numbers13 calls, $0.0410 total, $0.00315 average, $31.53 per 10,000 questions, $4.959 left, about 1,570 questions of runway.
TrapsA small sample. Outliers hidden by the average. A question mix that changes in real use. Caching, which lowers cost later.
Better habitQuote a range: the average case and the worst case (maximum and 95th percentile).
Budget levelsTotal, daily, per user, per feature, per request (max_tokens).
Ours vs. consoleOur BUDGET is an early warning that counts only this app. The Anthropic console is the truth.

Item 5 · Spend controls and credits

Two locks on the same door

One lock lives at the provider and one lives in your code. If one fails, the other still holds.

Two layersProvider layer: the console's monthly spend limit and email alerts. App layer: costs.py.
Console controlsA monthly spend limit (a hard stop), an email notification at an amount you choose, and the prepaid balance itself.
CreditsPrepaid and non-refundable. Anthropic's credit terms say usage credits expire one year after issue, and unused credits are lost if the account is closed. Confirm your exact date in Billing.
API keyA password that spends your money. Use one key per project, keep it out of code and chat, rotate it if exposed, and give it a clear name.
Who catches whatA leaked key used elsewhere: only the provider layer. A bug loop or one user spamming: the app layer, much sooner.
Rate limitsMaximum requests or tokens per minute. Exceed them and you get an error (covered under reliability).
ActionLower the default monthly limit to about $10, add an alert at $3 or $5, and use a separate key for the lab.
RememberDefense in depth: provider limits catch what your code cannot see.

Item 6 · Cost attribution

Who spent it?

A total of $50 tells you nothing. "$30 came from one user's broad questions" tells you what to fix.

What it isAnswering who or what caused a cost.
DimensionsUser, model, feature, team or tenant, question type, time. We cover user and time today.
How we did itA user column, the --user flag, the Users tab, and a per-user daily quota.
What it revealsIn the example below, User C asks the fewest questions but pays the most per question, which points to broad "summarise everything" requests.
UsesQuotas, abuse detection, chargeback, pricing, choosing what to optimise first.
LimitsNames are self-declared because there is no login. There is no feature tag. Shared costs are invisible.

RememberTag at the moment of the call. You cannot reconstruct who caused a cost afterwards.

Item 7 · Guardrails v1

Say no before you pay

A guardrail is a rule around the model. Version one is about money: a total budget, a daily cap and a quota per user.

Inside ask()

  1. Question arrives
  2. Check limits
  3. Blocked? Return, $0 spent
  4. Retrieve
  5. Call Claude
  6. Log cost
Three familiesCost (built), safety and quality (still to come).
Our rulesTotal $5.00, daily cap $0.50, user quota $0.25 a day, warning at 80%, answers limited to 500 tokens.
Fail fastCheck before the expensive step, and run the cheapest checks first.
Soft vs. hardSoft warns at 80% and still allows. Hard blocks at 100%.
WeaknessesOne call can overshoot, because we check spend so far. Two simultaneous requests can both pass. The user name is self-declared. The count lives in a local file.
Worked example$0.2499 spent of a $0.25 quota. The next question passes the check and costs $0.003, so the final spend is $0.2529.
Middleware ideaA chain of small checks every request passes through: auth, rate limit, budget, input safety, output safety, log.
RememberFail fast. Check before the expensive step, so a rejected question costs nothing.

Item 8 · Dashboard and observability

If you can't see it, you can't fix it

Observability means seeing what the system does and why, from the outside. A dashboard is one way to look.

Three pillarsLogs (we have them), metrics (we have them, as the dashboard), traces (missing). A trace shows which step of one request was slow.
AI metricsCost, speed, traffic, errors, quality, and retrieval health.
Our tabsOverview: on track? FinOps: where is the money going? Users: who? Guardrails: near a limit? Governance: what happened? Ask: try it.
How it worksusage.db to pandas to plotly to Streamlit. Every click re-runs the script from the top.
Dashboard vs. alertA dashboard is pull: you go and look. An alert is push: it tells you. Runaway bugs at night need alerts.
LimitsA local file, no auto-refresh, no traces, and demo data can mislead if left switched on.
Tools laterLangfuse, LangSmith, OpenTelemetry, Grafana. Building it by hand first shows what those tools do.
RememberA chart should answer a question someone will act on.

Test yourself

Eight questions, answers inside

1. Why does ingest cost nothing but ask costs money?

Ingest runs locally on your PC. Ask sends tokens to Claude, which bills per token.

2. Claude writes 1,000 tokens to a 1,000-token prompt. Which half costs more?

Output costs 5 times more at these prices: $0.005 against $0.001.

3. Why store the cost in each row?

Prices change. A stored cost stays correct for that day, while recalculating later would rewrite history.

4. Why is quoting only the average risky?

It hides spikes. A burst of heavy questions blows past a forecast built on the average.

5. A thief uses your key to call Claude directly. Which layer protects you?

The provider layer: the monthly limit and the alert. Your app never sees those calls.

6. What should you tag now to learn which feature is expensive later?

A feature column on every call.

7. $0.2499 of a $0.25 quota is spent and the next question costs $0.003. Allowed?

Yes. The check passes, and the final spend is $0.2529.

8. Which pillar shows whether retrieval or Claude was the slow step?

Traces. Logs and metrics show that a request was slow, not which step.
